The Real First Questions

Everything you're actually wondering.

Are they actually real?

Yes. Every coin sold through Kinzer Coins is a genuine ancient artifact — struck by real ancient mints, not a reproduction or cast. Many are third-party authenticated and slabbed by NGC (Numismatic Guaranty Company), the most trusted grading service in the world. Curious how experts spot a fake? Read Could You Spot a Fake? in the Journal.

Are they expensive?

Not the way people assume. Genuine ancient Roman bronzes start around $10. Silver denarii, Byzantine bronzes, and biblical prutot mostly sit between $30–$150. There are coins that cost thousands, sure — but the entry point is genuinely accessible.

Is it legal to own them?

Yes. Coins sold through Kinzer Coins are sourced from established dealers with clear provenance and comply with US and international laws governing private ownership of ancient coins.

Can I actually touch them, or do they need to stay in a case?

Both are fine. Bronze and silver coins that have already survived 1,500–2,500 years handle a bit of respectful contact just fine. Higher-grade coins are typically kept in NGC slabs or protective flips to preserve the surface, but the whole point is that you own it — hold it however feels right.

How do I know what I'm buying?

Every listing includes the ruler, the mint, the approximate date, the denomination, and the condition grade. NGC-slabbed coins come with the grading service's own certification. If a listing is missing something you want to know, ask — we'll tell you.

What should my first coin actually be?

A NGC-certified Roman bronze of a recognizable emperor — Constantine, Aurelian, Diocletian — in Fine condition, around $30–$45. You get a real emperor's portrait, clear enough to see the detail, at a price that lets you buy without stress. A Constantine bronze is where most people start.

For readers who know the story

History through coins.

You already know who Caesar was. You've read about Constantine, the tetrarchy, the fall. This is coins organized the way you already think about history — around people, events, and themes.

People

The rulers who held the die.

Every emperor, king, and general controlled their own coinage. The portraits are propaganda — but they're also the closest thing to a photograph any of these people ever had.

c. AD 306–337
Constantine the Great

Founded Constantinople. Legalized Christianity. Coins struck across the empire during his 30-year reign are among the most collected Roman bronzes.

See Constantine's coins
AD 270–275
Aurelian

Reunited the fractured Roman Empire — Gallic breakaway to the west, Palmyrene to the east. His coins bear the title RESTITVTOR ORBIS. He earned it.

See Aurelian's coins
AD 198–217
Caracalla & the Gladiators

The emperor featured in Gladiator II. Extended Roman citizenship to every free man in the empire. Read the Kinzer Journal on the coins of the arena and their emperors.

Read the Journal article
275–215 BC
Hieron II of Syracuse

Ruled Syracuse during Archimedes' lifetime and funded his engineering work. His bronzes carried the artistic peak of Greek Sicily.

See Hieron's coins
40–37 BC
Mattathias Antigonus II

Last king of the Hasmonean dynasty. Executed on the orders of Mark Antony to make way for Herod. His prutot were struck at the pivot point of Judaean history.

See Antigonus's coins
AD 802–811
Nikephoros I

Deposed Empress Irene, died fighting Khan Krum, and — famously — had his skull turned into a drinking cup by the victorious Bulgar khan. His folles survive him.

See Nikephoros's coins
